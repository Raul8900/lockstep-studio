## Plain reading copy

**THE TEAR** — I just tore a chunk off this QR code. It still scans. That's because most of it isn't your message at all.

**ATTEMPT 1** — So what's the rest? A backup copy? Smudge one letter, and the two copies disagree. Which one's right? Copies can't tell you. Reed-Solomon codes, from 1960, do something smarter.

**ATTEMPT 2** — Say your message is just two numbers. Two dots fix exactly one line. Now add extra dots on that same line. Tear any two away: the survivors still fix the line, and it puts the missing dots back.

**THE LIAR** — But a scanner can't see holes. To a scanner, a tear looks like a wrong number. So which dot is lying? The one that's off the line. Every liar costs two extra dots: one finds it, one fixes it.

**THE REAL CODE** — A QR code does the same trick, with bytes. This one has twenty-six bytes. Nine are your message. Seventeen are extra points. At two per liar, it can fix any eight. About thirty percent. That's why a logo can sit in the middle. It's damage, on purpose.

**THE FINE PRINT** — The catch: that's thirty percent of the bytes, not of the paper. These three corner squares aren't protected at all. They're how the scanner finds the code. Tear into one, and this code won't scan.

**THE FIRST TEAR** — That first tear? It took six of the nine message bytes. The extra points rebuilt every one. A torn QR code isn't lucky. It was built expecting to be torn.
