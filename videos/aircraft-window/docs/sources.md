# Engineering sources
Checked 17 November 2026. Original geometry and diagrams; no source illustrations copied.

1. Airbus Safety First, [Under the Spotlights](https://safetyfirst.airbus.com/under-the-spotlights/), “Cabin windows”, “A fail-safe structural part”. The article's embedded content was also retrieved via its own iframe URL. It explicitly states the outer pane sustains normal loads, the small inner-pane vent admits cabin pressure to the gap, and the inner pane can sustain differential-pressure loads if the outer pane fails. It identifies a separate transparent passenger-side protective lining.
2. UK AAIB, [Airbus A321-253NX G-OATW report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/661e3918d4a839725cbd3d78/Airbus_A321-253NX_G-OATW_06-24.pdf), printed page 5, “Cabin windows description”. The vent in the inner pane admits cabin pressure into the structural gap; both structural panes can carry the full differential pressure. The third scratch pane is attached to cabin trim, is touched by passengers, protects the inner pane and is not load-bearing.

Claim mapping: layer order/protective role → AAIB p5 + Airbus Cabin windows; inner-only vent/gap pressure → both; normal outer load → Airbus fail-safe section; inner backup → both. 35,000 feet is the requested illustrative cruise setting, not an asserted threshold. Enlarged gaps and vent are schematic, not dimensions. Backup failure is hypothetical; the real incident discussed in the sources concerned abnormal spotlight heat, which this film does not portray as normal operation. No universal aircraft-window claim or guarantee against all shattering is made. Public hook wording often foregrounds the mystery hole; this production instead gives the load-path distinction specified by the requester, without copying a creator's treatment.
