## Plain reading copy

**hook** — Every GPS satellite is launched with its clock set wrong. On purpose.

**phone** — Your phone finds you by timing radio signals from satellites twenty thousand kilometres up. Light covers thirty centimetres in a billionth of a second. So a clock that's a billionth off puts you thirty centimetres off.

**einstein** — But Einstein showed that time doesn't tick at the same rate everywhere. Up there, gravity is weaker, so the clock runs fast, by about forty-five microseconds a day. It's also moving at almost four kilometres a second, which slows it down by about seven. Net result: thirty-eight microseconds fast. Every single day.

**stakes** — That sounds like nothing. But at the speed of light, it's more than eleven kilometres. Ignore it, and your blue dot drifts that far off in a single day.

**doubt** — In 1977, not everyone believed it. So the Navy's test satellite, NTS-2, flew with the fix built in, but switched off. They'd only turn it on if Einstein turned out to be right.

**proof** — For about twenty days, the clock ran uncorrected. Einstein's math predicted how fast it would run. The clock matched him to within one percent. They flipped the switch.

**payoff** — Ever since, every GPS clock is tuned a hair slow before launch. Wrong on the ground, so it's right in the sky. And every time that blue dot finds you, Einstein passes the test again.
