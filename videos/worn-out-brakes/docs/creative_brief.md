# Creative brief — “They Wear the Brakes Out on Purpose”

## Audience and promise

Anyone who has flown, or watched a plane land from a window seat: they felt the shove forward and
heard the engines roar, and assume the roar is what stopped them. In about 55 seconds they learn the
three things that really stop an airliner (spoilers press it onto its wheels, reversers help at
speed, carbon brakes do the work by turning speed into heat) and the brutal test that proves the
brakes: Boeing machines them down to the last 2% of their life, loads the jet to maximum weight,
switches the reversers off and stops from 333 km/h. They leave able to say three things: the brakes
pass 1,370 °C, plugs in the wheels melt on purpose, and nobody may touch the jet for five minutes.

## Angle

Candidates:
1. **Worn out on purpose**: to certify a jet, Boeing grinds its brakes nearly to nothing, then makes them stop the heaviest possible take-off. A test that sounds insane.
2. **The roar isn't what stops you**: the reversers are loud, the brakes do the work.
3. **Wheels that let their own air out**: fuse plugs melt so a tyre deflates instead of exploding.
4. **Five minutes**: nobody may touch the jet, because that's how long fire crews take to arrive.
5. **385 megajoules**: a 737 at 333 km/h carries the energy of about 665 cars at 100 km/h.

Kept: 1 as the hook and spine, 2 as the setup (it gives the viewer the mechanism they came for:
"how do planes stop after landing" is a live search), and 3, 4 and 5 as the escalation inside the
test. Angle 2 alone needed the claim that landings are certified without reversers, which the
regulation I read (14 CFR 25.125) does not state outright, so it is not claimed. 1 wins because it is
a story with a test and a verdict, every number comes from Boeing's own 2026 write-ups of the 737-10
and 777-9 tests, and the hero (a brake) is natural to build in code.

## Story

The hero is one carbon brake on a 737's main wheel, built in code; the viewer is the passenger.
Line one is the provocation: Boeing grinds its brakes until they're worn out, then tries to stop the
plane with them. Setup, brisk: normally three things stop you, each seen happening on a landing jet
at night (spoilers flip up and the weight presses down, the reverser cowls slide back, then inside
the wheel the brake stack starts to glow). Escalation, "the test takes the help away": three stamps
(maximum weight, 2% left, no reverse thrust), the speed counts to 333 km/h, the brakes slam.
The peak, slow and held: 385 megajoules pour into four brakes; they go cherry, orange, white past
1,370 degrees; a plug in the wheel melts and the tyre sighs flat instead of bursting. Verdict: a
five-minute clock, fire trucks waiting at the edge of the light, steam; "all four brakes held".
Payoff: the camera rises from the glowing gear to a cabin window: the brakes under your seat have
survived worse than any landing, "because to certify a new jet," and the video loops into line one.

Beats: hook (the provocation), normal (three things stop you), test (the help taken away),
heat (energy, temperature, fuse plug), rule (five minutes, verdict), payoff (loop).

## Hook

1. Picture: a grinder biting into a stack of carbon brake discs, sparks; line: "To certify a new jet, Boeing grinds its brakes down until they're worn out."
2. Picture: a brake glowing white on a dark runway; line: "These brakes just passed 1,370 degrees. On purpose."
3. Picture: a landing jet, engines roaring; line: "That roar isn't what stops your plane."

Kept: 1. It opens the question "why would anyone do that?" inside two seconds, the grinder is an
arresting, physical picture, and the second sentence ("then it tries to stop the plane with them")
promises the test the video delivers in the test and heat beats. 2 spends the best picture too early;
3 leans on the reverser claim we dropped.

## World and hero

One world: a night airfield under sodium lights, built in code. A 737-style twin-jet (fuselage,
swept wings with spoiler panels that rise, two engines whose rear cowls slide back to reverse, main
gear with four braked wheels) rolling down a runway lined with edge lights; and, inside the main
wheel, the hero: a carbon brake stack of alternating discs on an axle, with its wear pin. Heat is the
only saturated colour in the film: the discs go from graphite to cherry, orange and white. Abstract
things become physical: lift is a field of upward arrows that collapses when the spoilers rise and
turns into weight arrows pressing on the wheels; energy is a stream of glowing particles flowing from
the moving jet into four brakes; the five minutes is a ring that counts down on the tarmac. No people
are built; the fire crew are three trucks with light bars waiting at the edge of the light. No real
photographs: the media hosts are out of reach from this machine (see Shot plan), and the brake test
footage that exists is Boeing's own copyrighted film, which the pipeline does not use.

## Shot plan

- hook: 3D close-up of the brake stack on a test bench, a cutting wheel sweeping across the disc edges throwing sparks (instances), slow push-in; overlay: a wear gauge ring counting down to 2% left. Then a whip to the whole jet sitting at the runway threshold under lights. Media: none (built in code).
- normal: night landing, low camera by the main gear as the jet rolls past. On "panels" the spoilers flip up and upward lift arrows over the wing shrink while weight arrows press onto the wheels (pointer label SPOILERS). On "reverse" a rear three-quarter chase: the reverser cowls slide back and streaks of air blow forward. On "carbon" the camera dives into the wheel: the brake stack, discs clamping, a faint red glow. Media: none.
- test: stamps slammed over a slow orbit of the jet at the threshold: MAX WEIGHT 89,765 kg, BRAKES 2% LEFT, REVERSERS OFF (one per phrase); then the take-off roll, camera tracking alongside, speed counter to 333 km/h; on "slams" a camera shake, tyre smoke and the nose dipping. Media: none.
- heat: stream of glowing particles from the rolling jet into four brake icons above it, counter to 385 MJ; cut inside the brake: discs heat graphite to cherry to white, thermometer number to 1,370 °C; then the wheel rim: the fuse plug melts, a jet of air, the tyre sags flat. Media: none.
- rule: the jet stopped, smoke and heat shimmer off the gear, a 5:00 ring counting on the tarmac, three fire trucks waiting at the edge of the light with lights flashing; stamp ALL FOUR HELD. Media: none.
- payoff: crane up from the glowing gear along the fuselage to a lit cabin window, then a hard cut to the first frame of the hook (the cutting wheel) for the loop. Media: none.

Gap: no photograph or film could be fetched (Wikimedia, NASA, Openverse and the Internet Archive are
refused by this machine's network), and the real test footage is Boeing's copyrighted film; the
video is animated in code throughout and says so nowhere it would mislead: nothing is passed off as
real footage.

## Pacing

Total about 56 s. hook 6 s, steady with a 0.4 s beat after "worn out". normal 11 s, brisk: one
sentence per mechanism, each seen as it is said. test 9 s at speed 1.08 with stamps on each phrase
and a 0.45 s hold on the brake slam. heat 11 s, the peak: held 0.4 s after the tyre line so the
deflation plays. rule 7 s with a 0.7 s hold on "all four brakes held". payoff 5 s, no tail: it runs
straight into the first line. The normal beat is kept brisk to pay for the held moments in heat and
rule; the video does not get longer.

## Look

`runway-night`: a sodium-lit night airfield, cool blue-grey shadows and warm orange runway light,
with heat (cherry, orange, white) the only strongly saturated colour, so the brakes are where the eye
goes. White captions with the key word in amber; overlays in amber (accent) and hot red (alert);
a light bloom so the glowing discs and runway lights read; vignette for depth; cuts by default, a
flash on the brake slam.

## Sound and voice

Voice af_heart at 1.15 (1.08 for the test beat so the three conditions land). Score from the engine:
drone under the hook, groove through the normal landing, build into the test, dark for the heat, tense
under the five minutes, drive for the payoff so it loops without a resolving chord. Effects: a
grinding rip and sparks under the hook, whooshes on camera moves, a thunk as the spoilers rise, a
swell for the reversers, stamps on each condition, a riser into the brake slam and an impact on it,
a hiss when the fuse plug melts, ticks for the countdown.

## What would make this fail

1. A cheap-looking plane: a tube with flat wings reads as a toy. Prevented by proper proportions
   (737 fuselage fineness, swept tapered wings, engines slung forward under the wing), night light
   that hides detail, and keeping the camera close on the gear and the brake, where detail is simple.
2. Numbers piling up (89,765 kg, 2%, 333, 385, 1,370, 5): each gets its own picture and only the
   three the viewer must remember get a held moment (2%, 1,370, five minutes).
3. Getting it wrong: "brakes do 100%" is true for the test, not a normal landing; the script says the
   brakes do "the real work" on landing and only claims "no reverse thrust" for the test. Every number
   traces to docs/sources.md.
