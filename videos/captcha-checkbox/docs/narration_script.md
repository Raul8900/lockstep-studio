## Plain reading copy

**THE QUESTION** — You tap a box that says you're not a robot. Researchers say the test started before your finger got there.

**THE OLD TEST** — For years, the test was reading wobbly letters. In 2013, Google said its risk analysis covers everything you do around a challenge, not only the answer you type. In 2014, the checkbox arrived, and many people passed with one click.

**THE TAP** — The click feels like the test. It's really a request for a verdict. The evidence was gathered around it. If it looks human, you pass. If it can't tell, you get a puzzle.

**WHAT IT WATCHES** — So what does it watch? Google doesn't publish the full list. A 2021 survey describes the checkbox recording cursor movement, where you click, and how long the pointer hovers. It also records browser details and cookies. Google's own FAQ calls a cookie essential to that analysis.

**THE CURSOR** — A real hand never moves at one steady speed. It speeds up, peaks, and eases into the target. In 1985, Flash and Hogan modeled arm movement this way: a smooth, bell-shaped curve of speed. One published detector checks cursor paths against a model like that. In a 2024 study of reCAPTCHA version two, any cursor movement cut the number of puzzles needed to pass. Straight paths and curved ones were not significantly different. So is it the wobble? Google doesn't say.

**THE BROWSER** — Then there's the browser. A 2024 study reports that cookies and browsing history carry substantial weight in whether a visitor seems human. Browsing history is what researchers report. Google hasn't confirmed it.

**THE SCORE** — Version three has no checkbox at all. It returns a score from zero to one. One means very likely a good interaction. Zero means very likely a bot. Each site sets its own cutoff, and Google suggests starting at 0.5. But a score can be pushed. In 2019, researchers trained an AI agent to move and click in a simulator they built. It reached a score of 0.9, about 97% of the time. The simulator isn't the live site.

**THE LIMITS** — A 2023 study ran for thirteen months with more than 3,600 people. Its authors judged the checkbox costly, with close to no security gain, and say it should be retired. Nobody outside Google knows how the signals are weighted. So every study tests from the outside.

**THE TAKEAWAY** — So the tap is the last step, not the test. It cares how you got there.

## Accuracy notes on the wording

- *“covers everything you do around a challenge”* (2013) — row 1: Google's 2013 post, paraphrased from a search excerpt.
- *“many people passed with one click”* (2014) — row 2: Google's 2014 launch post; the wording is a paraphrase.
- *“It's really a request for a verdict.”* — row 8: the click is among the data passed to the risk engine; row 2: the verdict falls back to a puzzle when unclear.
- *“If it can't tell, you get a puzzle.”* — row 2 (falls back to a puzzle when unsure) and row 8 (an image challenge follows when a visitor is not cleared).

- *“recording cursor movement, where you click, and how long the pointer hovers”* — row 8, a 2021 survey's description. Not Google's own statement.
- *“Google's own FAQ calls a cookie essential”* — row 4: the FAQ treats `_GRECAPTCHA` as essential to the risk analysis.
- *“A real hand never moves at one steady speed … bell-shaped curve of speed”* — row 10: Flash and Hogan's 1985 model of human reaching, with a smooth bell-shaped speed profile.
- *“One published detector checks cursor paths against a model like that”* — row 9: BeCAPTCHA-Mouse, a detector built on neuromotor-model features. Its data come from puzzle drags, not checkbox clicks; the screen says so.
- *“any cursor movement cut the number of puzzles needed to pass”* and *“Straight paths and curved ones were not significantly different”* — row 6: the 2024 test; the straight-versus-curved comparison has t = 0.58, p = 0.57.
- *“cookies and browsing history carry substantial weight”* — row 6: the authors' own report. *“Google hasn't confirmed it”* — no Google statement found in rows 1–4.
- *“Version three has no checkbox”*, *“one means very likely a good interaction, zero means very likely a bot”* — row 3: the v3 documentation.
- *“Google suggests starting at 0.5”* — row 3: Google's suggested starting cutoff; sites tune their own.
